# Urban context: Baghdad

Brief for "The City", the vision layer of the Amanat Baghdad prototype.
Researched 30 Oct 2026 by web search. Not field-checked.

## How to read this

- **verified (source)**: the figure appears in the named source. It is not independently confirmed beyond that.
- **unverified**: single weak source, conflicting sources, or not found. Do not show on the site without a label.
- Source numbers [n] refer to the list at the end.
- Rule for the prototype: any number on a City page needs a source line. Otherwise mark it "illustrative".

## Gaps to know first

- No current official figure was found for: share of sewage treated, water plant capacity, city area, the number of Amanat municipalities, the current mayor.
- Much technical data (sewage, water) is from 2006–2013 donor reports.
- Several news sites (IraqiNews, Shafaq) show page dates that do not match article content. Dates below use the article text where possible.

### The Tigris

- Flows north to south and splits the city into **Karkh** (west bank) and **Rusafa** (east bank). verified [6]
- Length inside the city: about 45 km (geographer quoted by AFP); 50–60 km in academic studies. unverified (range)
- Baghdad uses about 5 million m³ of drinking water a day. verified (Shafaq, Sep 2025) [7]
- Upstream dams in Turkey and diversions in Iran have reduced flows. verified (Shafaq) [7]

### Districts and governance

- Run by **Amanat Baghdad** (Mayoralty), mayor–council model. verified [3]
- Wikipedia names the mayor as Ammar Moussa Kadhum. Current holder in 2026: unverified. Turnover is frequent. [3][8]
- Number of municipal departments (بلديات): 15 per a Mustansiriyah University paper (up from 9 before 2003). unverified (single source) [9]
- Named municipal departments seen on the Amanat site: Sadr 1, Sadr 2, Rusafa centre, Karkh centre, Kadhimiya. verified (areq.net mirror) [10]
- Wikipedia says the city has 9 administrative districts. unverified [3]
- Traffic megaprojects are run by the national **Ministry of Construction, Housing, Municipalities and Public Works** and the PM's office, not Amanat. verified [11]

## 2. Current problems

### Tigris pollution and sewage

- Raw sewage flows into the Tigris along al-Nahr Street, between Medical City and Abu Nuwas Street. verified (Shafaq, Aug 2025) [12]
- More than 10 outlets seen, mostly between Bab al-Muadham and Karrada. verified (Shafaq, Aug 2026) [13]
- Sewage system treats "less than half" of daily wastewater. verified as a news claim (IntelliNews, Aug 2025; no named source) [14]
- Karkh plant: ~556,000 m³/day generated vs ~205,000 m³/day treated (~37%). verified, date unclear, likely 2010s [15]
- Before 2004 rehabilitation, no Baghdad sewage was treated. verified [16]
- Hospital wastewater from Medical City raises coliforms. Total coliform 200–3,700 /100 ml. verified (academic) [17]
- 2024 sampling: north and central sites "fair", south site "bad" on NSF index. verified (Dec 2025 study) [18]

### Water supply

- Demand: ~5 million m³/day. verified (2025) [7]
- Plant output: ~2.9 million m³/day from 11 plants; shortfall ~1 million m³/day. verified (World Bank, c. 2012) [19]
- Design 2.82 vs actual 2.34 million m³/day (2006). verified (JICA) [20]
- Iraq reserves below 8 billion m³ (Sep 2025). verified (Shafaq) [7]
- Current plant capacity: unverified (not found).

### Air quality

- PM2.5 7–10× WHO guideline during smog episodes. verified (AFP via Arab News, Jan 2025) [21]
- Government closed 111 brickworks and 57 asphalt plants; Nahrawan industrial zone named as main source. verified [21]
- Other sources named: private generators, refineries, Dora power plant. verified [21][22]
- Brickworks given 18 months to switch from heavy fuel oil to gas. verified [21]
- Iraq ranked 6th most polluted country by IQAir, 2023. verified (as cited by AFP) [21]
- No Baghdad PM2.5 source-apportionment study found after 2020. Gap.

### Heat and tree loss

- Record high 51.8 °C, July 2020. verified (multiple outlets; official IMO record not seen) [23]
- Green cover fell from >28% to ~12% over two decades. verified as quoted claim (lecturer Maryam Faisal, NYT, Feb 2023) [24]
- Baghdad planning director: loss "accelerated since 2003". verified [24]
- Green space per capita 0.02 m². unverified (secondary summary of a 2019 paper) [25]
- Surface temperature rose 8 °C between 2000 and 2015. unverified (single Landsat paper, date unclear) [26]
- Areas over 45 °C mapped from Landsat (Iraqi Journal of Science, 2023). verified [26]

### Traffic and the overpass program

- Mar 2023: national "first package" of 16 road, bridge and overpass projects. verified [11][27]
- Oct 2025: 14th of 16 opened (Al-Talae–Al-Bayjiya overpasses, 7,623 m). verified [11]
- Second phase includes the Krayat bridge. verified (Jul 2025 report) [28]
- Abu Nuwas St + Qadisiyah overpass + Second Jadriya Bridge: >88% done. verified (Ministry, Oct 2026) [28]
- No measured traffic outcomes found. Gap.
- Apr 2026 headline: projects face funding shortage. unverified (headline only) [28]

### Flooding

- Nov 2025: first rain flooded streets. Amanat blamed power faults; generator-run pump stations at 50% capacity. verified (Shafaq) [29]
- Combined storm and sewage networks overflow into homes. verified (older sources, 2010–2015) [29]
- Current sewer capacity: unverified.

### Waste

- Baghdad generates ~7,000–9,000 t/day. unverified (university page) [30]
- Per capita 0.6–1.0 kg/day. verified (2026 conference paper) [30]
- Nahrawan waste-to-energy plant awarded to Shanghai SUS Environment. Capacity 3,000 t/day; output reported as 45 MW or 100 MW. verified award; output unverified [30]
- Second plant planned for Abu Ghraib. verified [30]

### Heritage loss

- 1980s survey: 4,100 heritage houses in old east-bank districts; ~900 remain. verified as reported (Al Jazeera, date unclear) [34]
- 2022 estimate ~2,500 remaining. unverified (different baseline) [34]
- Owners flood or burn listed houses to get around demolition bans. verified (The National, 2022) [35]
- University of Leeds made a digital record of shanasheel houses. verified [34]

## 5. Vision material

### Precedents

| Place | What changed | Key figures | Lesson for Baghdad |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seoul, Cheonggyecheon | Removed 5.6 km elevated highway; restored stream (2003–2005) | Cost >₩386 bn (~US$281 m); 3.3–5.9 °C cooler than parallel road; biodiversity +639% (2003–2008); pumps ~120,000 t/day | verified [52]. Caveats: pumped water, land prices +30–50% within 50 m |
| Madrid Río | Buried M-30 ring road; park along Manzanares (competition 2005) | ~120–150 ha; ~€4.1 bn total, ~90% for road burial; 11–12 new footbridges | figures conflict, unverified [53] |
| Paris, Seine | Clean-up for 2024 Olympics; public swimming from 5 Jul 2025 | ~€1.4 bn; 3 bathing sites; ~100,000 swimmers in first weeks | verified [54] |
| Pontevedra, Galicia | Pedestrianised historic centre from 1999 | City claims: centre traffic −97%; zero road deaths for a decade (start year 2008–2011 differs); ~83,000 people | verified as city claims [55] |

- Other Galician examples (Lugo walls, A Coruña seafront): not researched here. To do.
- Baghdad's own precedent: Arthur Erickson's 1979–81 Abu Nuwas plan, ~3.5 km of Tigris frontage with museums, library, gardens. Never built. verified [56]

### "Baghdad could" ideas

Each idea is tied to a real place. All are aspiration. Label them as such.

1. **Abu Nuwas as a river park, not a road.** Ten km of riverfront exists already (verified [44]). Idea: shade trees, continuous walk, no through traffic, sewage outfalls on al-Nahr St closed first. Precedent: Madrid Río.
2. **Close the outfalls between Bab al-Muadham and Karrada.** Publish a map of each outlet and its closure date. Precedent: Paris Seine. This is the honest first step before any "swim in the Tigris" image.
3. **Rashid Street as a tram-and-walk street.** Phase work already covers facades (verified [41]). Idea: one tram or bus lane, arcades restored, no parking. Precedent: Pontevedra centre.
4. **Mutanabbi–Qishla–Saray as a pedestrian district.** Already pedestrian in part (verified [43]). Idea: link it to the river steps and Rashid St as one car-free quarter.
5. **Shade before towers.** Plant along walking routes to schools and markets, using the "Baghdad Plants" target (verified [46]). Show a Landsat heat map next to the planting plan.
6. **River islands as public nature.** Baghdad Tourist Island (~60 ha) and the Jadriya / Umm al-Khanazir areas, noted for biodiversity by UNDP (verified [57]). Idea: open, low-built nature parks reached by foot bridge and river boat.
7. **Zawraa Park as a connected green spine.** 3 km² (verified [47]). Idea: link it to the river and Karkh neighbourhoods with shaded walks instead of road edges.
8. **Heritage houses as homes.** About 900–2,500 houses remain (unverified range [34]). Idea: a public register and repair grants, so restoration beats demolition.

### Wording notes for the site

- Say "imagine" or "could" on vision content. Say "today" only next to a cited figure.
- Avoid "first", "largest", "world-class".
- Do not show Bismayah or overpasses as the vision. Show them only in "what is being built now", with a source.
